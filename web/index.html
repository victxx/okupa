The story

A pet that needs nobody's permission.

What

A cozy companion game. You feed it, play with it and draw for it. It hangs your drawings on its wall, dreams, makes little things and writes you letters. Its mind runs on 0G and decides what it does next.

Why

Ethereum promises that anyone can take part without asking. We wanted to test that at the very edge. If a 2009 handheld can find nodes, sign and hand over its own transactions, with no company in the middle, then anything can.

When and where

Built for ETHGlobal Mumbai, November 2026, from an empty repository. It runs on a real Nintendo DSi, on macOS and Linux, and in this page.

How it works

One small C core, everywhere.

Ethereum, peer to peer

The DSi finds nodes through Ethereum's DNS lists and discv4, does the RLPx handshake, speaks eth/69 and hands its signed transaction to each node it reaches. Nobody relays for it.

Its own key

The key is made on the console from your stylus scribbles, timed by the DSi's 33 MHz clock. It never leaves the SD card. Keccak, secp256k1, RLP and EIP-1559 all run on the ARM9.

A mind on 0G

Each decision is chosen from a closed set and signed by a TEE. The console checks that signature in C, over its own bytes, before acting. If 0G can't be verified, the okupa naps; it never pretends.

Did it land?

The core asks peers for new blocks, re-hashes each header and rebuilds the transaction trie to find its own hash. That is how the okupa checks inclusion without trusting anyone.
